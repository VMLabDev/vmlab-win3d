# The x86 D3D12 probe's first-run stall

Some acceptance runs (`tests/e2e/run.sh`) scored 11 of 12 because the x86
(32-bit) D3D12 probe presented one frame after tens of seconds, and its
window was in no console capture. The cause is not in the graphics stack.
In a fresh Windows guest, **Microsoft Defender Antivirus holds the first
load of each new unsigned image until its cloud protection service
answers**. That covers the probe's own executable and the package's
`vkd3dcore.dll` and `dxgi-vulkan.dll`, loaded at `D3D12CreateDevice` and
`CreateSwapChainForHwnd`. On helios's slow internet link each answer took 4
to 16 seconds, and the delays add up. The probe started its 20-second clock
at start-up, so when start-up took longer than 20 seconds it drew a single
frame, read it back and exited.

The fix is in the probes: `--seconds` now counts from the first frame, and
the result JSON reports `startup_seconds`. Nothing in the package changes.
The cost is a one-time cost per file per guest, and any unsigned program
pays it on first launch. Code signing with an established publisher
reputation is the real remedy, and that is outside this work
(`docs/decisions/0001-signing.md`).

The work was done on 2026-10-19 on helios: `AMD1` (RADV, Mesa 26.2.2) and
`Nvidia1` (NVIDIA 610.57.04), QEMU 11.1.1 (the patched QEMU on NVIDIA),
virglrenderer 1.3.0, vmlab 0.28.0-alpha, the `x86_64/windows-11@26100.1742.5`
template. The evidence is in `docs/evidence/x86d3d12/`.

## Result

With the fix (the package built from `a573717`):

| Check | AMD1 | Nvidia1 | Nvidia2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tests/e2e/run.sh --template windows-11`, fresh lab | 12 of 12; x86 D3D12 `startup_seconds` 7.2, 1194 frames | 12 of 12; `startup_seconds` 5.9, 1153 frames | 12 of 12; `startup_seconds` 2.2, 1157 frames |
| 50 iterations of the x86 then the x64 D3D12 probe (`src/loop.sh`, 20 s each, console captures every 2 s) | 100 of 100 pass; every run's window in 8 or more captures; x86 at 51.8 fps or more | not run (the card was draining for another worker) | 100 of 100 pass; every window in 8 or more captures; x86 at 30.9 fps or more |

The loops ran in the lab of each card's acceptance run, so each D3D12 DLL
had already been loaded once there. Their `startup_seconds` stayed under
4.1 s. The acceptance runs are the first-run case: their x86 D3D12 start-up
of 2 to 7 s would have cost the old probe that much render time, and now
it costs none.

Before the fix, the same loop in the lab of a passing AMD1 acceptance run
was also 100 of 100 (`loop-amd1-steady/`). The stall is a first-run effect,
and only a fresh lab shows it.

## The sightings

All four were the first D3D12 runs in a freshly cloned lab, and three
happened while the host was heavily loaded.

| Where | `elapsed_seconds` to the only frame | Run-Probe duration | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zeus, Intel, 2026-10-17 (load average about 35) | 73.3 | 82.9 s | `docs/evidence/zeus/intel/03-experiment/probes/x86-d3d12_probe.json` |
| AMD1, worker `wu`, 2026-10-18 (load average about 28) | 40.5 | 81.8 s | `docs/evidence/wu/e2e/windows-11-amd1-run1/probes/x86-d3d12_probe.json` |
| AMD1, worker `dd16`, 2026-10-18 | window not captured | not kept | `docs/evidence/dd16/amd1/baseline/e2e-results.txt` |
| Nvidia2, worker `dwmheap`, 2026-10-19 | 23.7 | 39.7 s | `sighting-dwmheap-nvidia2/x86-d3d12_probe.json` |

`frames_presented` was 1 each time for the same reason. The probe measured
`--seconds 20` from `probe_init`, so a start-up longer than 20 seconds made
the first frame the last one. Each of those runs re-ran clean in the same
lab. In the 27 other 20-second acceptance runs whose results are under
`docs/evidence/`, the x86 D3D12 probe rendered for the full time.

## Finding where the time goes

### The repro

`src/d3d12_phases.cpp` is `probes/d3d12/d3d12_probe.cpp` with a phase
trace. It writes the time of every step, measured from process creation, to
`<--out>.phases.txt` and flushes each line. A watchdog thread
(`src/phases.h`) scans every thread's stack when one phase lasts 5 seconds,
and again at 15 and 45 seconds. It reports the return addresses it finds as
module and nearest export. `src/build.sh` builds it for x64 and x86 with the
probes' toolchain and flags. It is installed under the name
`d3d12_probe.exe`, so vkd3d-proton's per-program shader cache file is the
same one the probe uses.

### Steady state: no stall in 100 runs

`src/loop.sh` runs the probe through `tests/guest/Run-Probe.ps1` in the
autologon session, as `run.sh` does, with console captures every 2 seconds.
In the lab of a passing acceptance run on AMD1, 50 iterations of x86 then
x64 at 20 seconds each all passed (`loop-amd1-steady/runs.tsv`). The mean
was 54 fps, every run showed its window in 6 or more captures, and the host
load average ranged from 13 to 41. The traces put device creation at 70 to
280 ms and the first frame 0.2 to 0.6 s after `main`.

Starving the VM of host CPU did not produce the pattern either. With QEMU
and the render server pinned to two host CPUs that four busy loops also used
(`src/pin.sh`, `src/burn.sh`), every phase slowed evenly: device creation
took 3.5 s, each fence waited up to 3.8 s, and the rate fell to between 1
and 19 fps. The first frame still came 5 to 10 s after start-up
(`loop-amd1-starved/`).

### Fresh labs: the stall, caught

`src/fresh.sh` runs the whole acceptance flow in a new lab with the repro in
place of both D3D12 probes, then saves the traces and the guest's Defender
logs. Three runs on AMD1 passed 12 of 12, and all three caught the delay at
smaller sizes than the sightings:

| Run | x64: `CreateSwapChainForHwnd` | x86: process creation to `main` | x86: `D3D12CreateDevice` | x86: `CreateSwapChainForHwnd` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `fresh-amd1-1` | 16.3 s | 16.1 s | 3.5 s | 3.0 s |
| `fresh-amd1-2` | 16.3 s | 12.0 s | 4.4 s | 5.7 s |
| `fresh-amd1-3` | 3.8 s | 6.9 s | 4.2 s | 4.0 s |

In the steady loops those steps take under 0.3 s, and under 0.1 s for
start-up to `main`.

Every stack scan shows the same main thread
(`fresh-amd1-1/probes/x64-d3d12_probe.json.phases.txt`):

```
thread 7132 (main) ip ntdll.dll+0x161704 (ZwCreateSection+0x14)
    ...  ntdll.dll  LdrLoadDll+0x170
    ...  KERNELBASE.dll  LoadLibraryExW+0xff
    ...  d3d12.dll+0x1e5a      the bridge's bridge_load_private_module
```

The bridge from `patches/vkd3d-proton/0005` loads its private DLLs from
`System32\vmlab-win3d\` when they are first needed: `vkd3dcore.dll` at
`D3D12CreateDevice`, and `dxgi-vulkan.dll` and `d3d12-native.dll` at swap
chain creation. The thread waits in `NtCreateSection` for the image
section. That is where a file-system minifilter, here Defender's real-time
protection, scans an image before the process can map it.

Defender's own logs line up with each wait to within 70 ms
(`fresh-amd1-3/load-evidence.txt`, `fresh-amd1-3/mplog-window.txt`). Each
Event 2010 ("used cloud protection to get additional security intelligence")
ends a cloud request, and each stalled step ends just after one, with times
in guest local time:

| Stalled step (repro trace) | Ended | Defender event 2010 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| x64 `CreateSwapChainForHwnd` | 17:40:42.388 | 17:40:42.331 |
| x86 process start (`main`) | 17:41:26.202 | 17:41:26.192 |
| x86 `D3D12CreateDevice` | 17:41:30.448 | 17:41:30.383 |
| x86 `CreateSwapChainForHwnd` | 17:41:34.447 | 17:41:34.392 |

The MPLog shows how the x86 run starts. Defender marks the x86 probe
executable for a `lofi/sample/expensive` scan
(`[RTP] [MpRtp] Engine VFZ lofi/sample/expensive: ...\win3d\probes\x86\d3d12_probe.exe`),
and makes cloud requests at 00:41:03, 00:41:11 and 00:41:19 (UTC) before the
process reaches `main` at 00:41:26. It makes the same request for every x86
probe executable and for the x64 bridge, `System32\d3d12.dll`, but not for
the x64 probes. That is one reason x86 is hit harder. The other is that x86
loads its own copies of every DLL for the first time. Cloud requests in
these labs took 4 to 12 seconds. The lab settings are the Windows defaults:
`MAPSReporting=2`, `SubmitSamplesConsent=1`, `CloudBlockLevel=0`,
`CloudExtendedTimeout=0`. Smart App Control is in evaluation mode
(`VerifiedAndReputablePolicyState=2`). Code Integrity logged nothing for the
package files.

Once Defender has a verdict for a file, it does not ask again in that guest.
The second run of each probe in a lab is fast, which is why reruns pass and
the steady loops never stall.

`src/Test-FirstLoad.ps1` maps each DLL as an image from SYSTEM
(`LoadLibraryExW` with `DONT_RESOLVE_DLL_REFERENCES`) before the probes run.
That took 0.4 to 14 ms per file, and the probes stalled afterwards just the
same (`fresh-amd1-2/firstload.txt`). Defender's cloud check is triggered
by the load into the user's process, not by mapping the file as SYSTEM. So
warming the cache from the installer, which runs as SYSTEM, would need more
than mapping the files.

The `wu` sighting also spent about 40 s after its only frame before writing
its result, which is teardown time. That was not reproduced here. It fits
another first-time image load after the first present, on the present path,
which swap chain release waits for. The phase trace will show it if it
happens again.

## The fix

`probes/common/probe.c`: the first `probe_last_frame` call of a run starts
the `--seconds` clock (`render_start`), so a probe renders for the full
`--seconds` however long start-up took. `probe_finish` writes
`startup_seconds`, the time from `probe_init` to the first frame
(`probes/README.md`). `elapsed_seconds` still runs from start-up to the last
present, so `avg_fps` keeps its meaning. `tests/e2e/run.sh` needs no change:
it captures the console every 2 seconds for as long as the probe process
runs, and `Run-Probe.ps1` allows `--seconds` plus 300 seconds.

Excluding the package from Defender, or turning cloud protection off, would
weaken the guest's protection for every user of the package, so neither was
done. Signing the DLLs with a test certificate does not help either: cloud
reputation needs a trusted publisher.

## What remains

- **The first launch of a D3D12 program in a new guest can wait on Defender**,
  once per new file: the program's executable, and the package's
  `d3d12.dll`, `vkd3dcore.dll` and `dxgi-vulkan.dll` per bitness. It took
  up to about 70 s in total on a slow link. Programs that are already known
  to Defender's cloud skip the executable's share. `docs/known-limits.md`
  records this.
- The other probes (D3D9, D3D11, OpenGL, Vulkan) load fewer new DLLs and
  were never seen past 20 seconds, but they get the same `--seconds`
  change.
- The 40-second wait after the first frame in the `wu` sighting is
  unexplained (see above).
